Infection Trail: Origin • Infected • Clean

You are the incident analyst for Northstar Health Services. An EDR alert indicates a suspicious executable may have moved laterally through the workstation network. Correlate host and firewall logs, reconstruct the timeline, and classify each endpoint based on evidence—not assumptions.
SOC Investigation Lab

1. Mission Brief

  • Find the origin: identify where the malicious chain begins based on the earliest confirmed execution.
  • Find infected hosts: look for evidence of successful execution or compromise after transfer.
  • Identify clean hosts: exposure alone does not equal compromise. Detection, blocking, or quarantine can prevent infection.
  • Correlate timestamps: the same network event can appear in multiple logs from different perspectives.
Analyst mindset: Do not classify a host because it merely contacted an infected machine. Ask: “What evidence proves the payload actually executed or was successfully contained?”

3. Classification Workspace

Classify every endpoint. Use one Origin. “Clean” means the available evidence does not establish successful compromise.

How to investigate: Start with the earliest timestamps. Follow the same filename/hash across systems. Match outbound connections to inbound connections. Then distinguish received, blocked/quarantined, and executed. Successful execution is stronger evidence of infection than detection alone.
